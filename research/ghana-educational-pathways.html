Ongoing Research · Social Policy & Educational Equity

Educational Pathway Disruptions in Ghana

Participation, progression, completion and re-entry, with attention to inequality and policy.

Status
Ongoing research, 2026–present
Stage
Research summary drafted; data and approach below are planned
Field
Social policy · Economic and Racial Equity concentration, Heller School, Brandeis University

Background

Educational participation and progression are important dimensions of social and economic opportunity. Although Ghana has implemented policies aimed at expanding access to education, challenges remain concerning students’ persistence, transitions between educational levels, completion, and re-entry following interruptions. Understanding these disruptions is important for identifying the circumstances under which educational inequalities emerge and persist.

Focus

This research examines educational pathway disruptions in Ghana, with particular attention to the transition from junior high school to senior high school. It explores how socioeconomic circumstances, gender, geographical location, and institutional conditions are associated with educational participation and progression. The study also considers the experiences of young people who leave formal schooling and the barriers and opportunities associated with returning to education.

Data and approach (planned)

Drawing on existing literature and nationally representative household survey data, the research seeks to identify patterns of educational inequality and examine their implications for educational policy and future livelihood opportunities. Particular attention is given to differences across population groups and the extent to which existing policies address barriers to educational continuity.

Objectives

The research aims to contribute to a better understanding of educational disruptions and inform evidence-based approaches to improving equitable educational progression and completion in Ghana.

Keywords: educational inequality · school transitions · educational disruption · school completion · re-entry · Ghana · social policy
